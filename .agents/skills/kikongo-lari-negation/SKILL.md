# Negation in Kikongo Lari

## Core pattern

Verbal negation is discontinuous: **negative marker + (subject particle) + verb + `ko`**, with an `a` linking vowel on the verb in the past/modal forms.

```
ka na zaba a ko      = je ne savais pas
ka wa zaba a ko      = tu ne savais pas
ka zaba a ko         = il/elle ne savait pas
ka ta zaba a ko      = nous ne savions pas
ka lua zaba a ko     = vous ne saviez pas
ka ba zaba a ko      = ils/elles ne savaient pas
```

## 2nd person singular: `ku` is correct

The negative marker for **tu** is `ku`, not `ka`. This is attested repeatedly in the translator and in user-provided corpus material. **Never "correct" `ku lendi ... ko` into `ka lendi ... ko`.**

```
ka ndendi a dia ko    = je ne peux pas manger
ku lendi a dia ko     = tu ne peux pas manger      <-- ku, not ka
ka lendi a dia ko     = il/elle ne peut pas manger
ka tu lendi a dia ko  = nous ne pouvons pas manger
ka lu lendi a dia ko  = vous ne pouvez pas manger
ka ba lendi a dia ko  = ils/elles ne peuvent pas manger
```

## Nominal negation

`Ka ... ko` surrounds the nominal predicate, with no `a` linker:

```
Ka muntu ko   = ce n'est pas une personne
```

## Rules to apply

1. Use `ku` for 2nd person singular negatives; `ka` for all other persons.
2. Keep the closing `ko`; it is never optional.
3. Keep the `a` linker in verbal negatives that carry it (`zaba a ko`, `dia ko` after `a`).
4. Never invent negative forms. Use only forms from the Jacquot & Lumwamu corpus or supplied by the user.
5. When user-supplied forms look irregular, keep them verbatim — do not normalize.
